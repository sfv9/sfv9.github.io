The Bid Path

How the government actually buys. Six stages, and what stalls each one.

Winning federal work isn't one big proposal. It's six stages, and most first deals stall on things that have nothing to do with the product: registrations, security approvals, and not having a contract the buyer can use. Here's the whole path in plain English.

We'll bid when the RFP comes out.
By the time an RFP is public, most of the decisions behind it are already made.
They loved the demo, so they'll buy it.
Liking the product isn't enough. They need budget, a contract to buy through, and approval to use it.
We need our own government contract first.
Most first sales go through a reseller or a prime contractor that already holds one.

Partners and resellers

How a reseller sale actually works

Contracting officers can generally only buy through contracts the government has already competed and awarded, called contract vehicles. If you aren't on one, a partner who is can sell your product for you. This is how most commercial software and hardware gets into government.

The order goes left to right. The money comes back right to left.
The agency
An end user has the problem. A program office holds the budget. Together they decide they want your product.
order
Contracting officer
The only person who can legally commit government money. Places the order on a vehicle the office can use.
order
Reseller / prime
Holds the vehicle (for example a GSA schedule, NASA SEWP or an agency BPA). Takes the order, handles the paperwork and invoices the government.
PO
You (the maker)
Supply the product and support under a reseller agreement. You usually still own the customer relationship and the selling.
Resellers fill orders. They rarely create demand.A reseller will process an order. They won't usually go out and find the buyer for you. You still do the selling, and they make it possible to buy.
Get on more than one vehicle.Different offices use different vehicles. Ask each buyer which one they use, then find a reseller or prime that holds it. Most makers have several resellers.
Expect a margin, and price for it.Resellers keep a share of the sale. Build that into your government pricing so you aren't squeezed, and keep your pricing consistent across partners.
